The "Pension Benefit Card" scam

4:23 min
Written by Caroline D'Cruz, journalist and editor with 16+ years of experience, founder of The Elders Club · Last updated 31 October 2026

Seeing ads or WhatsApp messages for a "Pension Benefit Card"? It is a scam. This guide shows how it works step by step, why it feels so real, four simple rules to protect yourself, and what to do if you have already shared details or sent money.

The steps, written out

1

What the ad or message promises

A Facebook or Instagram ad, or a WhatsApp message, says you can apply for a "Pension Benefit Card". It promises very high interest on a fixed deposit, free airport lounge entry, a free lifetime card and a free emergency loan.

On screen: An ad or WhatsApp message about a Pension Benefit Card

2

The scam, step 1: a link opens a form

The ad or message has a link. It opens a form that asks for your name, age and phone number, and sometimes your pension or bank details.

On screen: The form that opens from the link

3

The scam, step 2: the "approved" call

Someone phones you and says your application is approved. They sound helpful and make you feel safe.

On screen: A phone call saying your application is approved

4

The scam, step 3: they ask for bank details

The caller says you must give your bank account details to activate the card. Sometimes they ask for the OTP as well.

On screen: A request for bank details and an OTP

5

The scam, step 4: the money leaves your account

Once they have your details or OTP, money is taken from your account. Cases like this have been reported in the news in Pune and Ahmedabad, and police investigations are ongoing.

On screen: Money leaving a bank account

6

Rule 1: an ad or a forward is not proof

Just because someone sent it, or an ad is showing it, does not make it true.

On screen: The first safety rule

7

Rule 2: check the official way

To learn about a scheme or a card, go to your bank branch or the official website of that department. Do not click the link you were sent.

On screen: Checking with your bank branch or the official website

8

Rule 3: never share your OTP, PIN or bank details

Never give your OTP, PIN or bank account details to anyone who called or messaged you first.

On screen: Keeping your OTP and PIN private

9

Rule 4: a pension office does not ask for your OTP

A pension office does not usually phone and ask you to read out your OTP. If someone asks, something is wrong.

On screen: A caller asking for an OTP

10

If you shared details: tell your bank first

Do not panic. Call your bank straight away and tell them what happened. They can block your account or card.

On screen: Calling your bank

11

Then call 1930

Call 1930, the cyber crime helpline. The sooner you call, the better.

On screen: Calling the 1930 helpline

12

Then report it and keep your evidence

Report it at cybercrime.gov.in. Keep the link, the message, screenshots and the record of any money sent.

On screen: Reporting at cybercrime.gov.in

If something goes wrong

Is the "Pension Benefit Card" real?

The ads, messages and calls in this scam are not from your pension office. If you want to check any scheme or card, ask your bank branch or look at the official website of that department. Do not use the link you were sent.

Where has this scam been reported?

Cases have been reported in the news in Pune and Ahmedabad, where people lost lakhs of rupees. Police complaints have been filed and investigations are ongoing.

What if I only clicked the link?

Do not fill in the form or share any details. Close it, block the sender and report the message. If you did share details or send money, follow the three steps in this guide: bank first, then 1930, then cybercrime.gov.in.

What is 1930?

1930 is India's cyber crime helpline. Call it as soon as you realise you may have been cheated, after you have told your bank.

What should I do before I trust any offer?

Check it first. Go to your bank branch or the official website, and never share your OTP, PIN or bank details with someone who contacted you.

What you'll walk away knowing

By the end of this guide, made free and step-by-step by The Elders Club, you'll be able to:

  • Spot the fake "Pension Benefit Card" ads, messages and calls
  • Know the four steps of the scam
  • Know four rules that protect you
  • Know what to do if you have already shared details or sent money

Before you trust any ad or forward, check with your bank branch or the official website. And never share your OTP, PIN or bank details with someone who contacted you first.

Staying safe · Important

The video call that isn't really the police

2:51 min
Written by Caroline D'Cruz, journalist and editor with 16+ years of experience, founder of The Elders Club · Last updated 1 October 2026

Over ₹22,495 crore was lost to this one scam in 2025 — including an 86-year-old woman in Mumbai who lost ₹20 crore in a single call. This guide walks through exactly what a "digital arrest" video call looks like, why it works, and the one rule that exposes it every time.

The steps, written out

1

Know what this scam looks like

A video call comes in from a number you don't recognise. The caller claims to be a police or CBI officer and says your name has come up in a crime case.

On screen: A video call from an unknown number

2

Know the tactic: digital arrest

You're kept in front of the camera for hours, sometimes longer, and told not to speak to anyone - not even your own family - until the case is cleared.

On screen: Being kept on camera for hours

3

Know the one rule that exposes it

No real police force ever arrests you over a video call. A real arrest needs a warrant, delivered in person by an officer standing in front of you.

On screen: The no-arrest-by-video-call rule

4

Know why it works

No government department - police, CBI, or RBI - ever asks for money to prove you're innocent. The fear is the point; it stops you from stopping to think.

On screen: Why the fear tactic works

5

If this happens to you, don't panic

Hang up immediately. Never share your OTP, password, or UPI PIN with anyone on a call like this, no matter how real they sound.

On screen: Hanging up immediately

6

Report it straight away

Call the National Cyber Fraud Helpline on 1930 right away. The first hour matters most for actually getting your money back.

On screen: Calling the 1930 helpline

If something goes wrong

What is a digital arrest scam?

It's a scam where someone pretends to be a police or CBI officer on a video call, accuses you of a crime, and keeps you isolated on camera while demanding money to avoid arrest.

How can I tell a police video call is fake?

Real police never arrest you or investigate you over a video call. A genuine arrest always needs a warrant delivered in person by an officer.

What should I do if I'm already on a call like this?

Hang up immediately. Don't transfer any money, and don't share your OTP, password, or UPI PIN with anyone on the call.

What should I do after hanging up?

Call the National Cyber Fraud Helpline on 1930 straight away. Reporting it within the first hour gives you the best chance of recovering your money.

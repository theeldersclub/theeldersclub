Download & use Google Pay: send money safely

3:32 min
Written by Caroline D'Cruz, journalist and editor with 16+ years of experience, founder of The Elders Club · Last updated 2 September 2026

Sending money safely on Google Pay is one of the most useful things your phone can do — no more asking someone else to do it for you, no queueing at the bank. This guide walks through the entire process, from installing the app to completing your first payment, written for anyone trying it for the first time.

The steps, written out

1

Download Google Pay

Open the Play Store on an Android phone, or the App Store on an iPhone. Type "Google Pay" into the search bar and tap Install. It is free, and the icon is a small coloured pinwheel.

On screen: App store listing for Google Pay

2

Verify your phone number

Open the app and enter the mobile number that is registered with your bank account — this must be the same SIM that is in your phone right now, since Google Pay confirms it with a text message.

On screen: Phone number verification screen

3

Link your bank account

Tap "Add bank account" and choose your bank from the list. Google Pay finds the account linked to that phone number by itself — you do not need to type your account number.

On screen: Bank account selection screen

4

Set your UPI PIN

Using the debit card for that account, choose a 4 or 6 digit UPI PIN. This is the number you will enter for every single payment — it is not the same as your ATM PIN or net banking password.

On screen: UPI PIN creation screen

5

Send money with a phone number or UPI ID

Tap "Pay", then type the receiver's phone number or their UPI ID (it looks like a name followed by @ and their bank, such as ravi@okhdfc). Check their name appears correctly before you continue.

On screen: Send money to a contact screen

6

Or scan a QR code

At a shop, or when a family member holds up their phone, tap the scan icon and point your camera at the code. Many shops fill in the amount automatically — always check it is correct before you pay.

On screen: Scanning a QR code to pay

7

Enter your UPI PIN to finish

Every payment — whether it is ₹10 or ₹90,000 — ends with your UPI PIN. The moment you enter it, the money moves and a receipt appears on your screen.

On screen: Payment confirmation with receipt

8

Know what to never share

Your UPI PIN and any OTP are for your eyes only. Your bank, Google, and The Elders Club will never call or message asking for either one — anyone who does is trying to take money from your account.

On screen: Reminder: never share your PIN or OTP

If something goes wrong

Is Google Pay safe to send money with?

Yes. Every payment is approved with your UPI PIN, which is never stored on your phone or seen by Google or anyone else. The only way money can be taken from your account is if you say your PIN or an OTP out loud to someone — so never do that, no matter who is asking.

Does it cost anything to send money on Google Pay?

No. Sending money to a bank account through UPI on Google Pay is free, with no charge from Google or your bank, whether you send ₹10 or ₹90,000.

How much money can I send in one day?

Most banks allow up to ₹1,00,000 in UPI payments per day, across a maximum of 20 payments — though your own bank may set a lower limit. If a payment is refused, try a smaller amount or wait until the next day.

Does the person I'm paying also need Google Pay?

No. Google Pay uses UPI, the same payment system every Indian bank supports, so you can pay anyone with a UPI ID or bank account — even if they use a different app such as PhonePe or Paytm.

I sent money to the wrong person. What do I do?

Contact that person straight away and ask them to send it back, then call your bank's helpline the same day to report a wrong transfer. A completed UPI payment cannot be reversed automatically, so acting quickly matters.

I forgot my UPI PIN. How do I reset it?

Open Google Pay, tap your bank account, and choose "Forgot UPI PIN." You'll set a new one using your debit card details, the same way as the first time — no branch visit or phone call needed.

What you'll walk away knowing

  • Send money using UPI or phone number
  • Scan a QR code to make a payment
  • Know what never to share with anyone

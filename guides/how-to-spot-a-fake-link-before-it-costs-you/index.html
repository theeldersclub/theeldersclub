Staying safe · Important

Spot a fake link before it costs you

3:13 min
Written by Caroline D'Cruz, journalist and editor with 16+ years of experience, founder of The Elders Club · Last updated 24 September 2026

In Vadodara, a senior citizen clicked one link in a message — within half an hour, ₹7 lakh had disappeared from his bank account. This guide walks through exactly what a fake link looks like, what a genuine one looks like, and the one habit that would have stopped this before it started.

The steps, written out

1

Never click a suspicious link — not even to check

This one habit stops almost every version of this scam. If a message feels off, close it without tapping anything inside it — don't tap a link just to “see” where it goes.

On screen: A suspicious message, unopened

2

Know what a fake link looks like

Fake links are often short, with no clear website address, or have an extra word like “verify” or “KYC” added in — a real bank's website never has that.

On screen: Comparing a real link to a fake one

3

Every Indian bank's website now ends in .bank.in

Under a new RBI rule, every bank's real website ends in .bank.in. To find your bank's official site, search for it by name on Google rather than tapping a link, and check the address ends in .bank.in before you trust it.

On screen: Searching for a bank's official website

4

Watch for small spelling changes

An extra letter, a misspelled bank name, or a hyphen added into a web address is a common trick. If anything about the spelling looks even slightly off, don't click it.

On screen: A misspelled or hyphenated link

5

Don't let urgent words rush you

Real banks and government offices don't create panic. Words like “blocked,” “immediately,” or “suspended” are designed to make you act before you think — take that as a warning sign, not a deadline.

On screen: Urgent scam language

6

If you're not sure, call your bank directly

Don't call a number from inside the message itself. Find your bank's number from their official website, or from the back of your card, and ask them directly.

On screen: Calling a bank's official number

7

Government links only end in .gov.in or .nic.in

If a message claims to be from a government office and the link ends in .org.in, .com, or anything else, do not click it — only .gov.in and .nic.in are genuine.

On screen: Checking a government website address

8

If you've already clicked, don't enter anything

If you've tapped a suspicious link by accident, don't enter an OTP, password, or UPI PIN on whatever page opens. Close it immediately.

On screen: Closing a suspicious page without entering details

If something goes wrong

What should I do if I think I've been scammed?

Call the National Cyber Fraud Helpline on 1930 to report it as soon as possible — acting quickly gives you the best chance of recovering your money.

How do I know if a bank's website is genuine?

Search for your bank's name on Google rather than tapping a link, and check that the address ends in .bank.in — every Indian bank now uses this ending under a new RBI rule.

Is it ever safe to click a shortened link?

Only if you're completely certain who sent it and why. If there's any doubt, don't tap it — a shortened link hides the real website until it's too late to change your mind.

What if a message says my account will be blocked unless I act now?

That urgency is the scam, not a real deadline. Real banks and government offices don't rush you — close the message and contact your bank directly instead.

What you'll walk away knowing

By the end of this guide, made free and step-by-step by The Elders Club, you'll be able to:

  • Spot a fake link before you tap it
  • Recognise the new .bank.in rule for every Indian bank
  • Know that government sites only end in .gov.in or .nic.in
  • Know exactly what to do if you've already clicked

None of this requires remembering a long checklist — just one habit: never tap a link to “just check.” Search for the real website yourself, or call the number on your card, and you've defeated almost every version of this scam.

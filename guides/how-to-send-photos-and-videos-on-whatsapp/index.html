Share photos, your location, and a contact on WhatsApp

2:29 min
Written by Caroline D'Cruz, journalist and editor with 16+ years of experience, founder of The Elders Club · Last updated 16 September 2026

Sharing a photo, your location, or a saved contact on WhatsApp all starts from the very same button — the plus (+) icon next to where you type. This guide walks through all three, one at a time, plus how to turn on Location the first time you need it.

The steps, written out

1

Open a chat with the person you want to share with

From Chats, tap their name to open your conversation with them — sharing always happens from inside a chat, one person or group at a time, so there's no separate “sharing” screen to find first.

On screen: Open chat screen

2

Tap the plus (+) icon

Next to the message box, tap the plus icon to open the sharing menu — this is where Photos, Location, and Contact all live.

On screen: Plus icon sharing menu

3

To share a photo or video

Tap Photos on the sharing menu, choose a photo or video from your gallery, then tap the send button. You can add a short caption before sending, if you want to say something about it.

On screen: Choosing a photo to share

4

Turn on Location before you share it

Before sharing your location for the first time, make sure Location is turned on in your phone's settings — this step looks the same whether you have an Android phone or an iPhone.

On screen: Turning on Location in phone settings

5

To share your location

Tap the plus icon, then Location. Your phone finds where you are on its own — you don't need to type or search for anything. It appears in the chat as a small map, showing exactly where that pin is.

On screen: Sharing your current location

6

To share a contact

Tap the plus icon, then Contact, and choose the name you want to share from your phone's contact list. You can choose more than one name at once if you want to share several contacts in the same message.

On screen: Choosing a contact to share

7

Know who can see what you share

A photo, location, or contact you share is only visible to the person or group you sent it to in that chat — never posted publicly or shared with anyone else automatically.

On screen: Reminder: only shared in that chat

If something goes wrong

What's the difference between sharing my location once and “live” location?

This guide shows sending your current location — a single snapshot of where you are right now. WhatsApp also has a separate “Live location” option that keeps sharing your location as you move, for a set time you choose, then stops automatically.

If I ever turn on live location, how do I stop it?

Open that chat and tap “Stop sharing,” then confirm. It also stops on its own once the time you chose runs out — nothing keeps sharing forever.

Who can see the photo, location or contact I share?

Only the person or group you sent it to, inside that one chat — it is never made public or visible to anyone else.

Does sharing a contact share their phone number too?

Yes — a shared contact card includes whatever details are saved for them, usually their phone number, so only share a contact with someone you'd trust with that number.

Can I share more than one photo at a time?

Yes — after tapping Photos, you can select several photos or videos together before tapping the send button, rather than sending them one by one.

What if I don't want my phone finding my location afterwards?

You can turn Location off again in your phone's settings once you're done — it only needs to be on at the moment you want to share where you are.

What you'll walk away knowing

By the end of this guide, made free and step-by-step by The Elders Club, you'll be able to:

  • Send a photo or video to someone on WhatsApp
  • Share your current location so someone knows where you are
  • Share a saved contact with someone else
  • Know exactly who can see what you share

Once you've shared a photo, a location, or a contact this way, the next one takes just a few seconds — it's always the same plus icon, no matter which of the three you're sending.
